# Simplifier — Rev 4 second pass

Scope: only the two Rev-4 additions (DEC-018 stall/timeout constraint; AC1.1 composability clause). The four cuts from pass 1 (Success Criteria, COLLEVITY_HOOK_ERRLOG, stale-rollout caveat, AC2.1 "verbatim") are not revisited.

## 1. Catalog of new material
- Constraints → Accepted risks, third bullet: "A same-machine stall bounds delay, it does not block (DEC-018)" — one long paragraph.
- AC1.1: added clause — hook entry "coexists" with other `UserPromptSubmit` hooks, appends rather than replaces.

## 2. Challenge
**AC1.1 composability clause: keep, no cut.** It's one sentence, directly testable, and guards a real regression class (a naive installer overwriting the settings.json array and silently deleting someone else's hook). This is a requirement, not infrastructure-for-hypotheticals — reject as a cut candidate.

**DEC-018 paragraph: over-specified, not over-scoped.** The underlying requirement — bounded timeout keeps a stall non-blocking — is sound and worth keeping. But the prose re-derives its own conclusion three times: "modest explicit timeout" (title clause), "the worst-case wait is small" (mid), "keeps any worst case small either way" (end). That's the same claim stated three ways in one paragraph. It also narrates root causes of a hypothetical stall ("iCloud file materialization, or advisory-lock contention behind D3") — plausible causes, not something the AC or an implementer needs to enumerate to satisfy the requirement. And it closes on a hedge about Claude Code's own documentation gap ("silent on whether a timeout is specifically treated as blocking") — that's a decision-log rationale, not a spec constraint; the *spec* only needs the outcome (non-2 exit, including signal-kill, is non-blocking per AC4.1's existing contract).

## 3. Minimum viable cut
Collapse DEC-018 to: "The hook writes synchronously; a stall therefore delays rather than fails submission. No internal watchdog is added (DEC-015); the installed entry carries an explicit bounded `timeout` (see AC1.1/INSTALL.md) so worst-case delay is small, and a timeout-kill is non-blocking under the existing exit-code contract (AC4.1)." That's ~60 words vs. ~180 — same guarantee, no repeated conclusion, no stall-cause taxonomy, no meta-commentary on Claude Code's documentation gaps.

## 4. Over-specification flags
None beyond the above. AC1.1's coexistence clause states a requirement ("don't clobber the array"), not a solution — fine as-is.

## Verdict
One genuine trim available (DEC-018 prose, not its substance). AC1.1's new clause is lean and load-bearing — no change proposed there. Otherwise Rev 4 is appropriately scoped for v1 plumbing; no new structural cuts beyond the prose tightening above.
