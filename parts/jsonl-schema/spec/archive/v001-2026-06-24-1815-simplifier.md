## 2. Challenge pass

**Constraints — "Offset correctness is owned at capture, not here"**
This bullet is doing two things: stating a design principle *and* pre-explaining AC4.4's legacy-EDT/MDT detail. The principle belongs in Constraints. The EDT/MDT detail is spec-level AC content already in AC4.4. The second half of this bullet ("Excel is the one channel...") duplicates AC4.4 and adds reading friction. Candidate trim: cut the second sentence; AC4.4 is self-contained.

**AC1.5 + AC1.7 — optional field catalog**
AC1.5 names the four optional fields. AC1.7 specifies conventions for three of them. The split is logical but a reader must hold both ACs open simultaneously to understand any one optional field. This is a readability cost, not a functional one. Not a cut candidate — the MECE requirement forces the split — but flagging it as a known friction point if the spec is handed to an implementer cold.

**AC5.1 — staleness prose**
"Freshness is a consumer-composition contract" is well-motivated, but the sentence "staleness is not surfaced or enforced by the seam" is defensive explanation, not a testable requirement. A test cannot fail on its absence. It belongs in a comment or ADR note, not a leaf AC. Candidate trim: move to a parenthetical note or DEC reference, keep only the testable assertion ("read_day makes no standalone freshness guarantee; the consumer calling `sync_sources()` then `read_day()` owns the composition").

**Phase descriptions**
Both phase "Delivers" paragraphs are detailed enough to be implementation notes. They provide good orientation but drift toward solution prescription. At spec-seal they are harmless; at implementation they may over-constrain the builder. Low priority — acceptable as-is for a single-developer context.
